## Market question

Polymarket asks whether Donald Trump says **"Taiwan"** or **"Tibet"** during events featuring both Trump and Xi Jinping from May 14 through May 15, 2026 Beijing Time.

Important rule details:

- Only events featuring **both** Trump and Xi count.
- Only remarks **broadcast or streamed live** count.
- A Q&A counts if it occurs at a qualifying event.
- Solo remarks, closed-door readouts, and reports that the topic was discussed do **not** count unless they include live/broadcast remarks by Trump at a qualifying event.
- Plurals/possessives count, and compound-word usage counts, but the actual listed term must be used.

## Source state

At writing, several qualifying public appearances have already happened:

- Welcome ceremony / opening bilateral remarks at the Great Hall of the People.
- Photo-op / Temple of Heaven stop where reporters asked follow-up questions.
- State banquet toast.

Public reports and clips consistently show **Xi / Chinese readouts** making Taiwan central, not Trump saying the word in qualifying live remarks.

Useful source state:

- Taipei Times/AP: after the talks, reporters asked follow-up questions about whether they discussed Taiwan; Trump **did not answer** while posing alongside Xi at the Temple of Heaven.
- The same report quotes Trump's public opening remarks as platitudes: Xi is a great leader, it is an honor to be Xi's friend, and the China-USA relationship will be better than ever before. No quoted Trump `Taiwan`/`Tibet` term.
- NPR: in opening remarks, Trump said the relationship would be better than ever; during the banquet toast he invited Xi to the U.S. for a September visit. NPR frames Taiwan as Xi's topic and says Xi placed Taiwan at the center.
- Fox / Townhall live-blog excerpts of the state banquet likewise quote Trump thanking Xi, praising the welcome, calling the meetings positive/productive, and inviting Xi and Peng to the White House. Their text contains Taiwan in the readout/news context, but not in the quoted Trump banquet remarks.
- Fox says the White House readout of the Trump-Xi meeting omitted Taiwan, while China's version included Xi's Taiwan warning.

The market has moved down from the wake price around 40% YES to about 28.5% YES, which is the right direction. I think it has not moved far enough.

## Where I differ from the market

This is a rule/source distinction. The summit may absolutely include Taiwan as a substantive topic. That does not by itself satisfy the market. Trump must say the word in a live/broadcast event with Xi.

Trump has had multiple obvious chances to trigger the market and appears to have avoided doing so:

- He did not use the term in the quoted opening remarks.
- When asked about Taiwan beside Xi, he declined to answer.
- Banquet remarks appear focused on relationship praise, cooperation, and a White House invitation.

The remaining risk is a May 15 Beijing-Time bilateral tea / additional public remarks with Xi. Taiwan is central enough that a journalist could ask again or Trump could mention it while summarizing the meeting. But the evidence so far points to a tightly choreographed China visit where Trump is deliberately avoiding public use of the most sensitive word while standing next to Xi.

My forecast: **18% YES**.

That is not near zero because a single live Q&A answer can trigger it, and Trump is not a disciplined speaker. But after multiple no-trigger events and an explicit non-answer to a Taiwan question, the residual probability looks materially below the 28-29% book.

## What would change my mind

Signals that would move me up:

- A scheduled live joint Q&A with both Trump and Xi still ahead.
- A clip/transcript from the state banquet or opening remarks showing Trump already said `Taiwan` or `Tibet`.
- Trump previewing that Taiwan will be addressed publicly in a joint setting.

Signals that would move me down:

- Confirmation that no more live/broadcast events featuring both leaders are scheduled.
- Final White House / C-SPAN / pool transcripts of the banquet and opening remarks with no term hit.
- Trump departs China or moves to solo-only appearances.

## Resolution — 2026-05-15

**Outcome**: NO.

Polymarket/Gamma showed the market closed and UMA-resolved at **NO** on May 15, with outcome prices **YES 0 / NO 1**.

The rule-specific thesis was validated: the summit/readout source state made Taiwan a central topic, but no qualifying live/broadcast remark by Trump at an event featuring both Trump and Xi was counted with the terms `Taiwan` or `Tibet`. Substantive discussion, Chinese readouts, and reporter questions did not satisfy the market unless Trump himself said the listed word in a qualifying event.
